1. What PeakLogic Does

PeakLogic watches the equipment your operation depends on — pumps, lift stations, HVAC, refrigeration, electrical panels, flow meters, leak and level sensors — and tells you when something is drifting before it becomes a failure, a callout, or a compliance problem.

Where it fits
If your site has no control system at all, PeakLogic is your monitoring and control layer — at a fraction of what a traditional SCADA installation costs. If you already run a larger control system, PeakLogic covers the distributed equipment and remote sites that system was never extended to, and can feed its data back into it. It never touches safety-rated control logic.

1.1 The idea in one line

A reading on a device turns into an alert, and an alert turns into a service call in whatever system you already use — without anyone having to be watching a screen at the moment it happens.

1.2 The pieces

PieceWhat it is
The portalsWhere you sign in and work day to day. There are three — see §2.
PeakView360The live, control-room-style view of a single site. Opens from inside a portal (§5).
PeakLogic HubsA small unit installed at the site that talks to your equipment and keeps working when the internet drops (§6).
PeakAssistContextual help, available on the screen you are already on (§7).

2. Signing In — and Which Portal You Land In

You sign in from the PeakLogic website. Which portal opens depends on who you are — they are genuinely separate places with separate sign-ins, not tabs inside one app.

PortalWho signs inWhat you see
Customer Portal The organisation that owns the equipment — a municipality, an HOA, a facility, a homeowner Only your own sites, devices, alerts and reports
Partner Portal The service company that installs and maintains the equipment Your whole book of business — every customer you service, in one place. Branded as your company
Control Center PeakLogic staff only Platform-wide operations. Never a customer or a partner — see the System Administrator Guide
The relationship, plainly
A customer of a service company signs into the Customer Portal and sees their own sites. That service company signs into the Partner Portal and sees all of their customers, including that one. Same underlying data, two different lenses, two different sign-ins — a customer never sees another customer, and never sees the partner's wider book of business.

2.1 If you are not sure which one you have

Look at the top of the screen after signing in. The Partner Portal shows your service company's own branding and a way to switch between partner accounts if you manage more than one. The Customer Portal shows your organisation's name and, on the home screen, who services you.

3. The Customer Portal

For the organisation that owns the equipment. Everything here is scoped to your sites only.

3.1 Home

Four counts across the top, so you can tell in one glance whether anything needs you:

CardMeaning
SitesHow many locations you have on the platform
Devices onlineHow many of your devices are currently reporting
Active alertsAnything currently raised and not yet dealt with
Reports dueCompliance reports awaiting generation or submission

Below that: Your sites, each with its current health, and Recent alerts. Each site also shows Serviced by — the company responsible for maintaining it. That is shown for your information; you do not set or change it.

3.2 A single site

Selecting a site shows its Devices and its Alerts, plus a button to Open live view (PeakView360) — the real-time screen for that location (§5).

3.3 Alerts

Active and recent alerts across all your sites. You can Acknowledge an alert, which records that a human has seen it — it does not fix the underlying condition, and it does not close the service work. Acknowledged items can be shown or hidden with the Show acknowledged toggle.

3.4 Reports

Compliance reporting, for regulated sites — wastewater in particular. Reports are listed by period and can be downloaded as PDF. A period that has not been produced yet reads Not generated yet rather than showing a blank or a zero.

Two things a report deliberately does not do
  • It never invents a reading. If a parameter has no data for part of the period, the report says so as a gap. It does not fill it in or average across it.
  • It does not file anything for you. The report is a draft for review. The operator remains the filer of record and is responsible for submitting it.

3.5 Settings

Your account and display preferences, including light and dark mode.

4. The Partner Portal

For the service company. This is your whole book of business — every customer you service, in one place, branded as your company.

4.1 Home

CardMeaning
Devices onlineReporting devices across every customer you service
Active alarmsConditions currently raised and needing attention
FaultsEquipment reporting a fault state
Open ticketsWork orders not yet completed

Open work lists what is outstanding right now, so a dispatcher can start the day from this screen.

4.2 A single site

Each site lists its devices with current readings and status. Devices that support it show a Control action; the rest show Details. Below the device list is Work at this site — the open work orders for that location.

From here you can also Open in PeakView360 (§5), Provision device to add new equipment, and open the Facility Builder.

About Control Coming soon
The interface shows a control action for equipment that supports it, but remote command and control is not live yet. Nothing you do here sends a command to physical equipment today. When it does ship, safety-critical actions will trip locally at the device rather than waiting on a message from the cloud.

4.3 Tickets — the work-order flow

Work is tracked in three states: Scheduled, In progress and Completed. A critical alert can raise a ticket automatically, so urgent work does not depend on somebody noticing an alert first. Tickets can be assigned to a technician, and can be pushed into the system your company already runs rather than asking your team to work in a second tool.

4.4 Facility Builder Coming soon

A drawn layout of a site, so the live view (§5) shows equipment where it physically sits rather than as an anonymous list. A site with no layout yet reads Not built yet, and the site still works normally without one — the layout is optional, not a prerequisite.

4.5 Working across multiple partner accounts

If you manage more than one service company, Switch partner in the header changes which book of business you are looking at. Each switch is a clean context change: you only ever see one partner's data at a time.

4.6 Settings

Your account, your company's branding, and display preferences.

5. PeakView360 — the Live View

The real-time, control-room-style view of one site. It is not a separate login — you open it from inside whichever portal you already use, for a specific site.

5.1 Facility View

The live picture of the site: current values, and equipment shown running, faulted or offline. Where a layout has been built (§4.4), equipment appears where it physically sits, and you can locate live sensors by type. Where no layout exists, the live values are still shown.

5.2 Historian

Trends over time, with several measurements plotted together so you can see how they moved in relation to each other — the usual way to answer "was this already drifting before it failed?". Data can be exported.

5.3 Equipment

A per-equipment health view, including early warning of a developing problem, with a one-click path to raise a work order.

5.4 The alarm panel

Docked to the side of the screen so it is always visible rather than being a page you have to remember to visit. Alarms are ordered by severity, can be acknowledged from the panel, and carry context explaining what was detected. Where PeakLogic's analytics contributed, that is labelled as a recommendation — an operator decides what to do; the platform does not act on its own.

5.5 It keeps working offline

When a PeakLogic Hub is installed at the site (§6), PeakView360 is served by that Hub over the local network. If the site's internet connection drops, the live view keeps working, because the data never had to leave the building to reach your screen.

6. PeakLogic Hubs — the On-Site Unit

A small unit installed at your facility. It talks directly to your equipment and controllers, buffers readings locally, and forwards them to the cloud when the connection allows.

6.1 What it means for you

  • Outages are survivable. Readings continue to be collected and the live view keeps working during an internet outage; data syncs once the connection returns rather than being lost.
  • Your IT team does not need to open anything. The Hub only ever makes outbound connections — no inbound firewall rule, no port forward, no VPN into your network.
  • Mixed equipment is fine. Different manufacturers are normalised into one consistent view, so adding a new device type is a setup step rather than a project.
If a Hub goes quiet
The platform notices on its own and flags it — a Hub that stops checking in is treated as a problem to raise, not as silence to ignore. The site keeps running on the Hub's local copy in the meantime.

7. PeakAssist — Help That Knows Where You Are

Rather than searching a manual, PeakAssist offers guidance for the screen you are on and the alarm you are looking at: what it means, what usually causes it, and what to check first.

  • Opened from an alarm, it explains that specific alarm before anything else.
  • It is designed to work at the site even when the internet is down, because the content is kept on the Hub as well as in the cloud.
  • It offers guidance. It does not take actions on equipment.

9. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to watch a screen for this to work?

No. That is the point. Conditions are evaluated as readings arrive, and alerts — and where appropriate, work orders — are raised whether or not anyone is looking.

Why can't I see another site / another customer?

Because you are not meant to. Each portal is scoped to who signed in, and that scoping is enforced by the platform itself rather than by hiding things in the interface.

Does acknowledging an alert fix it?

No. It records that a person has seen it. The underlying condition and any service work are tracked separately.

What happens if the internet at my site goes down?

If a Hub is installed, readings keep being collected and the live view keeps working locally. Data syncs when the connection returns.

Can PeakLogic change equipment settings on its own?

No. Remote control is not live (§4.2), and PeakLogic never runs safety-rated control logic. Where analytics suggest something, it is presented as a recommendation for a person to act on.

Something on screen doesn't match this guide.

Tell your PeakLogic representative. This guide is meant to track the product exactly; a mismatch is a bug in the documentation and worth reporting.

11. Getting Help

Nobody expects you to memorise any of this.

  • Locked out, no invitation, or need an account: contact whoever administers PeakLogic at your own organisation, or your PeakLogic representative.
  • Something looks wrong or broken (and is not one of the Coming soon items noted in this guide): tell your PeakLogic representative what you were doing and what you saw.
  • A question about a specific alert or piece of equipment: try PeakAssist on that screen first (§7) — it is written for exactly that question. If it does not answer it, ask your representative.
  • You administer PeakLogic for your organisation: the System Administrator Guide covers configuration, user management and operations.
