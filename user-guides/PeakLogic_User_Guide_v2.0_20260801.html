1. Frequently Asked Questions

Short on time? Start here. Every answer links to the full section if you want more detail.

What is PeakLogic?
It's a website that watches your equipment for you — pumps, coolers, pool systems, and more — using small sensors. Instead of someone walking around checking things by hand, the sensors send readings automatically, and PeakLogic tells you the moment something looks wrong. It's growing into a bigger operations platform — with live equipment screens, on-site hubs that keep working even if the internet drops, automatic work orders, and compliance reports — but the day-to-day app in this guide works exactly as described. See Your Dashboard.
How do I log in?
Go to the web address your PeakLogic contact gave you, enter the email and temporary password you were emailed, then follow the on-screen prompts to set your own password. See Logging In for the First Time.
I forgot my password. What do I do?
On the login screen, click "Forgot your password?" and follow the instructions — you'll get a code by email to reset it. If that doesn't work, see Getting Help.
Why does it ask me for a 6-digit code every time I log in?
That's your second login step (sometimes called "two-factor" or "MFA") — it's a security requirement, not optional, and it's there to keep your account safe even if someone else guessed your password. See Setting Up Your Second Login Step.
What do the red, amber (orange), and green colors mean?
Green means everything's fine. Amber/orange means something's worth a look soon. Red means it needs attention now. This color pattern is used everywhere in the app — sites, equipment, and alerts all use the same three colors. See What the Colors Mean.
What's the difference between a "Site," an "Asset," and a "Device"?
A Site is a physical place (like your restaurant on Main Street). An Asset is a piece of equipment at that place (like the walk-in cooler). A Device is the small sensor attached to that equipment, which is what actually talks to PeakLogic. See the Glossary for more everyday examples.
How do I connect a new sensor?
Go to the Devices page and click "Onboard device." It's a short 2-step form — type in the serial number printed on the sensor, then tell it which site and piece of equipment it belongs to. Takes about a minute. Full walkthrough: Connecting a New Device.
I got an alert. What should I do?
Open the Alerts page, find the alert, and read the message — it tells you exactly what's wrong in plain language (e.g. "Temperature sensor offline — no data for 3 h"). If it's red (critical), treat it as urgent. See Alerts.
I clicked a button and nothing happened. Is something broken?
Probably not — a handful of buttons (like "Add site," "New ticket," and "Acknowledge") are still being built and don't do anything yet. This guide marks every one of them with a Coming soon tag so you always know in advance. Everything else in the app works normally.
Can I use PeakLogic on my phone?
Yes — it's a website, not an app you download, and the pages resize to fit a phone or tablet screen. Just open the same web address in your phone's browser and log in the same way.
Can other companies see my data?
No. Every customer's information is kept completely separate at the database level — not just hidden in the screen, but structurally walled off so it isn't possible for your data to mix with anyone else's.
Who do I contact if I'm stuck?
See Getting Help at the end of this guide.

2. Getting Started

2.1 What You'll Need

  • A computer, tablet, or phone with an internet connection and a web browser (Chrome, Safari, Edge, or Firefox all work fine)
  • The web address for your PeakLogic account (your PeakLogic contact will give you this)
  • The email invitation you received with a temporary password
  • Your phone, with an authenticator app installed (like Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator — both are free) — you'll need this once, during setup
You don't sign yourself up
There's no "Create Account" button anywhere in PeakLogic — on purpose. Your account is created for you by an administrator (either at your own company or at PeakLogic), and you're sent an email invitation with everything you need to get started. If you haven't received one yet, see Getting Help.

2.2 Logging In for the First Time

  1. Open the web address you were given, in any browser.
  2. You'll see the PeakLogic sign-in screen. Enter the email address your invitation was sent to.
  3. Enter the temporary password from your invitation email exactly as it appears (these are case-sensitive, so double-check capital letters).
  4. Click Sign In.
  5. Because this is your first time, you'll be asked to choose a new password. Pick something only you know, type it twice to confirm, and continue.
  6. Next, you'll be walked through setting up your second login step — see below, this only takes a minute.

2.3 Setting Up Your Second Login Step

PeakLogic requires a second login step beyond just your password — a fresh 6-digit code every time you sign in, generated by an app on your phone. This is standard practice for protecting business data and only needs to be set up once.

  1. Open the authenticator app on your phone (Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator both work — install one free from your phone's app store if you don't have one yet).
  2. On your computer screen, you'll see a small square barcode-like image called a QR code.
  3. In your phone's authenticator app, tap the button to add a new account (usually a + icon), then choose "Scan a QR code."
  4. Point your phone's camera at the QR code on your screen — the app will automatically add "PeakLogic" to your list.
  5. Your phone will now show a 6-digit number that changes every 30 seconds. Type that number into the box on your screen.
  6. Click Confirm. You're all set — from now on, every time you log in, you'll open the same app on your phone and type in whatever 6-digit code it's currently showing.
Keep your phone handy
You'll need this authenticator app every single time you log in from now on, so keep it installed. If you ever get a new phone, set up the authenticator app again before your old phone is wiped — otherwise contact support to get re-linked.

2.4 Logging In After That

Every visit after your first one is quicker:

  1. Go to the same web address and enter your email and the password you chose.
  2. Open your authenticator app and type in the current 6-digit code when asked.
  3. You're in — you'll land on your Dashboard.

2.5 Signing Out

Look at the bottom-left corner of the screen, underneath the navigation menu. You'll see your email address and, next to it, a small door-shaped icon — that's the sign-out button. Click it any time to log out.

7.2 Connecting a New Device

This is the one setup task you'll actually do yourself, and it's fully working today — no waiting required. When you get a new sensor from PeakLogic, here's how to bring it online:

  1. Click Devices in the left menu, then click the purple "Onboard device" button in the top-right corner.
  2. You'll land on a simple 2-step form. Step 1 asks for the serial number — this is printed on a label on the back of your sensor, in the format PLG-0000. Type it in (it doesn't matter if you use capital or lowercase letters).
  3. Click Next. PeakLogic checks the serial number for you — if it's valid and hasn't already been claimed by someone else, you'll move to Step 2 automatically. If something's wrong, you'll see a short message explaining what to fix.
  4. Step 2 asks where this sensor lives. Choose the Site from the dropdown list first.
  5. Once you've picked a site, a second dropdown appears for Asset — choose which specific piece of equipment this sensor is attached to. (This step is optional — you can skip it and assign it later if you're not sure yet.)
  6. Click Finish (or "Skip for now" if you didn't pick an asset). You'll see a green confirmation screen summarizing everything.
  7. From there, you can click "Onboard another" if you have more sensors to add, or "View all devices" to see it in your full device list.

12. Glossary — Plain-English Definitions

No jargon left unexplained. If you come across a word anywhere in PeakLogic and aren't sure what it means, it's probably here.

TermIn plain English
SiteA physical place — a restaurant, a pumping station, a pool facility. The "where."
AssetA specific piece of equipment at a Site — a pump, a fryer, a cooler. The "what."
DeviceThe small physical sensor attached to an Asset that actually measures and reports data. The "how."
TelemetryA fancy word for "the readings sent by a sensor" — temperature, power draw, flow rate, and so on.
AlertA notice that a reading crossed a threshold worth knowing about.
Service TicketA tracked repair or maintenance job, often created automatically from a critical alert.
Onboarding (a device)The process of connecting a brand-new sensor to your account for the first time.
Serial numberThe unique code printed on a physical sensor, used to identify it when connecting it.
FirmwareThe built-in software running on the sensor itself (not something you usually need to think about).
Two-factor login / MFAThe second login step — a 6-digit code from an app on your phone, in addition to your password.
TenantAn internal PeakLogic term for "your company's account" — you won't see this word in the app itself, but you might hear it from support.
Drill-downClicking from a Site, into one of its Assets, into one of that Asset's Devices — each click narrows down from "which location" to "this one exact reading."

13. The Wider Platform

What you have been reading about so far — Sites, Assets, Devices, Alerts and Tickets — is the part of PeakLogic most people use every day. It is one part of a larger platform. This section explains the other parts in plain language, and is honest about which ones you can use today.

Where PeakLogic fits
PeakLogic monitors the equipment your operation depends on — pumps, lift stations, HVAC, refrigeration, electrical panels, flow meters and leak sensors — and tells you when something is drifting before it becomes a failure. If your site has no control system at all, PeakLogic is your monitoring and control layer. If you already run a larger control system, PeakLogic covers the distributed equipment and remote sites that system was never extended to, and can feed its data back into it. It never touches safety-rated control logic.

13.1 PeakView360 — the operator screen Coming soon

A live, control-room-style view of a facility: current readings as large tiles, a docked alarm panel you can acknowledge from, equipment health dashboards, an interactive facility view showing where each sensor physically sits, and a multi-pen historian for comparing trends over time.

Status: all five screens are built and can be demonstrated, but they currently run on preview data. They are not yet connected to your live site.

13.2 PeakLogic Hubs — the on-site unit Coming soon

A small computer installed at your facility that talks directly to your equipment and controllers, keeps working when the internet drops, and syncs safely to the cloud when the connection returns. It only ever makes outbound connections, so your IT team never has to open a firewall port.

What this means for you: when a Hub is installed, your screens keep working during an internet outage, because the Hub serves them locally.

Status: the on-site software and the cloud side that manages it are both built. No Hub has been installed at a customer site yet.

13.3 Work orders & dispatch Coming soon

Today, a critical alert can automatically create a service ticket (§9). The wider platform extends this into a full work-order flow — dispatched, accepted, on-site, completed — so you can see not just what was raised, but what actually got done, and how long each stage took.

If your service work is handled by a partner company, work orders can be sent into the system that partner already uses, rather than asking them to log into another tool.

Status: built behind the scenes; no screen for it yet.

13.4 Compliance reporting Coming soon

For regulated facilities — wastewater in particular — PeakLogic can assemble a reporting period's readings into a draft compliance report, including minimums, maximums, averages and any exceedances.

Two things this deliberately does not do
  • It never invents a reading. If a parameter has no data for part of the period, the report says so as a gap rather than filling it in.
  • It does not file anything for you. The report is a draft. The operator remains the filer of record and is responsible for reviewing and submitting it.

Status: report generation is built; automatic delivery is not.

13.5 PeakAssist — contextual help Coming soon

Help that knows where you are. Instead of searching a manual, PeakAssist offers guidance for the screen you are on and the alarm you are looking at — what it means, what usually causes it, and what to check first. It is designed to work even when the site is offline.

Status: the help content and the logic that selects it are built; the in-app panel is not.

Why we list things that aren't ready

14. Getting Help

Nobody expects you to memorize any of this — if you're ever stuck, reach out.

  • Locked out, no invitation email, or need a new account: contact the administrator at your own company who manages your PeakLogic account, or your PeakLogic representative directly.
  • Something in the app looks wrong or broken (not one of the Coming soon items already noted in this guide): contact your PeakLogic representative with a description of what you were doing and what you saw.
  • A question about a specific alert or piece of equipment: your PeakLogic representative or your own facilities/maintenance team can help interpret it.
  • Lost your authenticator app / new phone: contact support to get your two-factor login reset before you're locked out.
